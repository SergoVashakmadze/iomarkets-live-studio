## Status at the end of Nov 1 (what is deployed, what the logs say)

Deployed from `fix/gapless-audio`, now merged to `main`: Teresa and MuseTalk on an L40S with 8 cores,
long sentences cut into pieces of at most 11 words (`live.py` `_even_parts`), the first clip held back
by `buffer_seconds` (1.1x speech + 1 s per piece, capped at 8 s), sentence audio scheduled on the
audio clock with the picture following it (`index.html` `schedule`/`syncAnchor`), and the page
sending numbers-only `[playback]` lines to the logs.

What the logs showed (about 60 renders):

- **Rendering is the floor**: about 1.0-1.5x the speech plus about 1 s per piece, whatever the
  hardware. An H100 gave no gain; 8 cores gave none either. So she cannot start for about 8-11 s
  after the reply begins without a break mid-speech; that wait is the delay testers will notice.
- **After the first words the voice no longer runs dry** (no `gap_ms` lines in the last session).
- **Not tried**: render the pieces in parallel on several GPUs (each piece's start frame is
  computable from the clip lengths, so the order does not matter); stream the anchor's text so the
  first piece is voiced while the rest is still being written; a shorter first piece.
- **Testers' computers**: the operator's 6-year-old laptop ran Chrome at over 100% CPU with the
  studio page open; ask testers to close other tabs.
- Read the logs with:
  `modal app logs iomarkets-live-studio --since 30m --timestamps | grep -E "\[speak\]|\[playback\]"`.
  `holding Ns` is the hold; `gap_ms` is a break on the page.
- The GPU is kept warm only by `bash deploy/warm.sh on N` (about $2.45 an hour); otherwise the first
  page load wakes it and the first reply waits about 2 minutes.

### Tester 1: Dingxin Pan (GPCR pharmacology), Nov 1

Consent form received, signed and dated 01 Nov 2026, countersigned (kept outside the repo, with the
recording folder the tester shared by email).

- **Worked:** no technical problems; the anchor knew the guest's background, had done its
  research, and asked specific questions; it understood the rationale of the drug-discovery approach.
- **Scientific terms were hard to follow:** P2Y1 receptor, knockout (KO) mice, Rac1, small G proteins.
  **Fixed (PR #40):** the voice's tokenizer (`voice/server.py`, `Phonemizer.__call__`) drops every
  digit, and `speech_text.to_speech` left letters and digits in one name unsplit, so "P2Y1" was said
  "P Y" and "Rac1" "Rac". `to_speech` now says each part (`_mixed`): "pee two why one",
  "Rac one", "jee pee tee four", "three dee", and decades ("the nineteen nineties"). Fourteen
  life-science words went into `LEXICON` (kinase, GTPase, phosphorylation, allosteric, CRISPR, mRNA,
  pharmacokinetics...). "KO" is "kay oh"; "GPCR" is spelled out. Before each interview, run
  `~/.cache/iomarkets-voice/bin/python voice/check.py --file brief.txt` on the guest's brief: it
  lists the words the voice would guess, and suggests sounds that need checking by ear before they
  go into `LEXICON` (its guess for "kinase" was "kinnuss").
- **Tone felt abrupt, questions truncated:** the even pieces of at most 11 words (`live.py`
  `_even_parts`) and the wait for lip-sync clips can break a question mid-thought. Next: compare
  the pieces against the anchor's full line in `[speak]` logs for this session, and see whether
  the anchor prompt asks for short questions (`agents/anchor.py`); a one-clause lead-in before the
  question would make it more conversational.
